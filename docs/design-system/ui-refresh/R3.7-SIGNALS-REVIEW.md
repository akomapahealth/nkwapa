# R3.7 follow-up: Persistent BP signals

The owner asked for an enduring risk highlight alongside the existing popup,
with broader stratification to follow later, and requested a bounded use of the
remaining context. This change implements reading-level BP signals without
inventing a composite patient-risk algorithm.

## Direction and boundary

Three options were considered: a badge alone (compact but easy to overlook), a
persistent reading panel (keeps value and meaning together), and a patient-wide
risk score (requires an agreed model and more than one vital). The implementation
combines the first two. The entry form gets a persistent number/unit/category
panel and five-band strip; saved history and read-only measurements get compact
tags. The existing popup still interrupts a saved Crisis reading.

The five bands and tones already exist in the app. Stage 1 is warning, Stage 2
severe and Crisis destructive; Normal/Elevated stay quiet. The selected band has
a visible check and `aria-current`, so color is supplementary. The component is
static, responsive and adds no interactive/focus controls. Category changes use
a polite live region; numeric keystrokes do not appear in that region.

The shared classifier and low-BP predicate are reused. The presentation guard
rejects missing/non-finite/out-of-bounds or reversed/equal pairs using the existing
shared limits. Low BP is flagged separately and suppresses a Normal/Elevated badge
and selected hypertension band. Mixed high systolic/low diastolic can show both
existing signals. No thresholds, validation, saves, API, schema, auth, sync,
outbox, replay or acknowledgement logic changed.

Entry explicitly identifies unsaved BP versus BP matching the locally saved
reading. “Saved BP” does not claim the entire form is saved or synchronized.
The saved-history tag belongs to that record and its existing provenance, not
to an inferred current patient-wide risk state.

Current guidance distinguishes BP categories from broader cardiovascular risk
assessment: [2025 AHA/ACC guidance](https://professional.heart.org/en/science-news/2025-high-blood-pressure-guideline/top-things-to-know).
This is a conceptual reference, not a new clinical protocol or a migration of the
app's locally approved classifier, escalation thresholds or vocabulary.

For a later low/medium/high model, specify the decision it supports, required
inputs, missing/stale-data rules, time horizon and clinical approval before
mapping it into this UI. Keep immediate escalation distinct from long-term risk.
Do not turn a normal single vital into a “low-risk patient” tag.
